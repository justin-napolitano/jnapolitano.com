I created my GitHub repo, [jnapolitano.com](https://github.com/justin-napolitano/jnapolitano.com), to showcase my personal projects, thoughts, and experiences. Think of it as my digital business card or a curated portfolio. I wanted a space where I could express myself, share my code, and connect with others in the developer community. 

## Why I Built This

The motivation behind this project is simple: I needed a platform to display my work and facilitate networking. A static website fits my needs perfectly. I wanted something lightweight, fast, and easily maintainable. Social media can be fleeting and chaotic, so having a central hub where anyone interested can find me was a priority.

### Goals:
- **Showcase projects**: I wanted to highlight my coding projects with real context.
- **Share insights**: I’m often working through problems or discovering new tech. It seemed only right to document and share those lessons.
- **Establish a presence**: Being a part of the developer community goes beyond just working on projects. I wanted to be findable.

## Key Design Decisions

Building a personal site sounds easy, but there are a few choices I put extra thought into:

### Simplicity Over Complexity
I’m a fan of minimal design. Users shouldn’t have to sift through unnecessary clutter to find what they need. This is a showcase, not a gallery. I focused on clean layouts and clear navigation.

### Static Site Generator
After considering different frameworks, I settled on using a static site generator (SSG) - specifically, [Eleventy](https://www.11ty.dev/). Here’s why:
- **Performance**: A static site is super fast; it loads quickly and is easily cached.
- **Flexibility**: I can use templates and layouts to preserve my preferred structure without extensive configuration.
- **Simplicity**: I wanted a tool that wouldn’t add to my overhead or complexity.

### Responsive Design
In today's world, mobile access to sites is paramount. I ensured my site is responsive, making it accessible on any device. Flexbox and CSS Grid made this less of a headache than I anticipated.

## Tech Stack and Tools

Here’s what’s under the hood:

- **Eleventy** for the static site generation
- **HTML5 & CSS3** for markup and styling
- **Sitestyles**: I used a light custom CSS because I wanted a unique feel without getting too bogged down in frameworks.
- **Netlify** for hosting: My site’s on a great CI/CD platform, making deployments seamless.

### How It’s Structured
- **src/**: All source files live here. This includes markdown files for posts and various layouts.
- **_includes/**: Contains reusable components like headers and footers.
- **css/**: My stylesheets, simple and straightforward.

## Tradeoffs

No project is without its challenges. Here are a few tradeoffs I faced along the way:

- **Static vs. Dynamic**: There’s something restrictive about static sites. If I need to add something dynamic later, that may lead to a heavier lift.
- **Scalability**: While everything feels efficient now, I wonder how well it would perform if I expanded significantly. Careful considerations will be needed when adding more posts or features.
- **Learning Curve**: I had to get used to Eleventy's idiosyncrasies and Markdown rendering. It was a touch different than usual frameworks like React or Vue which I generally use.

## What I'd Like to Improve Next

I love this site as a foundation, but there are always things to enhance. Here’s what's on my radar:

- **Blog Functionality**: Expanding the blog section. I want to engage more with the community by writing tutorials or sharing tips.
- **Search Feature**: Users need to find things quickly. A search functionality would enhance usability.
- **Accessibility**: I want to make sure the site is usable for everyone, so I plan to conduct an accessibility audit.
- **Dark Mode**: It seems to be a trend. I want to enable users to switch between light and dark modes seamlessly.

## Conclusion

The journey of creating my personal website, [jnapolitano.com](https://github.com/justin-napolitano/jnapolitano.com), has been rewarding. It gives me a place to polish my skills and cultivate relationships. I’m excited about the future improvements I have planned and can’t wait to share more.
